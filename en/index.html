01

Brand

THMAlt Duvenstedt · Since 1996

Lightness is not the goal.
It is the result
of getting the design right.

THM is a carbon component company founded in 1996 by Thomas Mertin and Petra Mertin in Alt Duvenstedt, a small village in Schleswig-Holstein, northern Germany. An aerospace engineer by profession, Thomas saw an opportunity in the fact that high-grade carbon technology from aviation was barely used in sport and leisure.

The breakthrough in cycling came with what were then the world's lightest crankset and fork. Raced and proven by the Deutsche Telekom pro team, these parts were named THM-Carbones. To this day THM does everything itself, from design and tooling to testing and production, in its own factory in Alt Duvenstedt.

Engineering

Lightest for real riders.
The lightest parts, for people who actually ride.

At THM, lightness does not start with cutting weight. We first analyse the path the load takes, then lay the fibres along it. Plenty where it is needed, nothing where it is not. That is why THM parts are so light, yet most are approved for a total weight of rider, bike and luggage of up to 110kg.

Every product is tested on THM's own custom rigs to levels far beyond the applicable safety standards. Working closely with materials researchers in Germany and elsewhere, THM builds prototypes with production methods no one has tried yet and feeds the results straight back into its products.

DesignLoad path first

Load path before form. Geometry and layup are defined on a precise load analysis, and the weight follows as a result.

ProcessTHM-RTM

Resin transfer moulding (RTM) raises tolerancing, consistency and strength-to-weight. It also has less environmental impact than traditional carbon fabrication.

FactoryBuilt by hand

Every product is built by hand at the factory Am Sportplatz in Alt Duvenstedt. Almost every team member in Germany is directly involved in engineering and production.

NamingA carbon skeleton

Collarbone (Clavicula), calf bone (Fibula), shinbone (Tibia), forearm bone (Ulna), jawbone (Mandibula). Every THM product is named after a bone in Latin.

Founders · Thomas & Petra Mertin

“A no-compromise approach. That is why builders who need the lightest and strongest components choose THM.”

Aerospace engineer Thomas Mertin found, in cycling, a niche where high-grade carbon parts were needed but no one was making them properly. Founded together with Petra, THM began with cranks and forks and has grown to include brakes, stems, handlebars and seatposts.

THM's Scapula fork was the first carbon fork built with continuous fibres running from the steerer all the way to the dropouts. Conceived with Formagic, a Cologne-based design studio, it went from CAD drawings to CNC-cut models, and every tool needed to make it was designed and built in-house by THM.

The company joined Italy's 3T in 2016, and since 2020 has been part of Schmolke Carbon, a fellow German carbon component maker. Through these changes production, sales and customer support have remained independent, and every product is still made in Alt Duvenstedt.

Cranksets

Clavicula SE · DM · XCFull carbon, axle included

Clavicula sine exceptione.
No rule without exception.

SE stands for the Latin sine exceptione, without exception. The Clavicula SE is THM's top-line crank, its geometry and layup redefined on a precise analysis of load paths. Extraordinarily stiff in every plane, it transmits your power without loss, and weighs 293g with spider.

THM is particularly proud of the patented connection between the left crank and the axle. It is the first to successfully put a carbon-to-carbon interface into production at the heart of the drivetrain. Aligning the fibres of the external and internal splines reduces wear to almost nothing and eliminates noise.

Clavicula SE Power Meter

The lightest power meter

Strain gauges, a small electronic module and a rechargeable lithium-ion battery all sit inside the carbon axle. The power meter adds under 27g, for a total of 320g compact and 329g standard.

Measuring from the left leg, it records power, cadence, torque effectiveness and pedal smoothness and transmits over ANT+ and Bluetooth. Accuracy is ±2%, a charge lasts around 150 hours, and it is IP67 waterproof. Power meter versions are made to order with long lead times, so please ask Rubi Workshop in advance.

Clavicula DM

Not just about low weight, about performance

The Clavicula DM uses the 8-bolt road direct mount interface. You can pair it with SRAM original chainrings, the SRM PM9 8-bolt spider, Wolf Tooth Aero, Gemini Rigel and many other chainrings, spiders and power meters for this standard.

The 29mm cylindrical carbon axle clamps on the non-drive side, adapting flexibly to the frame's bottom bracket shell. Total weight is under 260g.

03

Rim Brake

Fibula120g for the pair

For more than a decade,
the lightest brake.

For more than a decade, THM's Fibula rim brake has matched the performance of the top models from major brands at half their weight. Today's Fibula is redesigned for the era of wide rims, and gets its performance by fully exploiting the properties of carbon fibre.

The new Fibula is made by resin transfer moulding (RTM) for better tolerancing, consistency and strength-to-weight, and even the return spring is composite. At 120g for the pair, it is still the lightest brakeset available.

Swap between race and training wheels

An adjustable cam fits rim widths from 19mm to 30mm without moving the cable clamp, and with no effect on lever feel. Ideal for a bike that switches between wide race wheels and narrow training wheels, with room for tyres up to 25mm.

Tibia

A stem that does not cut the fibres

Most carbon stems drill through the fibres and add threaded inserts for the bolts. Tibia solves this with new clamping hardware made of titanium and aluminium. With fibres running from end to end, it is the stiffest and strongest stem in the lightweight class.

A streamlined silhouette inspired by natural forms, and a faceplate made of carbon down to its smallest structural elements. Now ICR ready, please confirm D-shape steerer and headset specifications when ordering.

Frontale Aero

Slim, yet stiff

The Frontale Aero combines elegant aero design with excellent lightweight construction. The NACA profile of the top is extremely slim, yet gives nothing away in stiffness or weight.

Drop 127mm, reach 75mm, 7° flare and 3° sweep. Widths from 38cm suit riders who want a narrow bar, and it comes in the same seven decal colours as the Clavicula. Computer mount sold separately.

05

Seatpost

MandibulaComfort zone · Power zone

A very light,
very comfortable seatpost.

The goal was simple, achieving it was hard. After two years of development THM released the Mandibula. Comfort seatposts share one problem: the farther they stick out of the frame, the more they flex. Taller and heavier riders end up on a softer post.

The Mandibula divides the post into two zones. The power zone is a stiff, light structure that hardly flexes however much is exposed, and the comfort zone gives exactly the right amount of comfort without becoming too much. Thanks to THM's layup, the saddle stays level through the flex.

Story

1996 ~ 2026Alt Duvenstedt, Germany

From an aerospace engineer's workshop
to the benchmark for the lightest parts.

From 1996, when an engineer in northern Germany began making cranks and forks from carbon, to today and the direct mount Clavicula. For 30 years THM has stayed true to a small team that designs and builds everything itself.

1996

THM Founded

Thomas and Petra Mertin found THM in Alt Duvenstedt. The starting point was bringing aerospace carbon technology to sport and leisure.

1996 ~

THM-Carbones

THM builds what are then the world's lightest crank and fork, proven in racing by the Deutsche Telekom pro team. This is where the name THM-Carbones comes from.

2003

Am Sportplatz

With steady growth, the company moves into its present factory Am Sportplatz in Alt Duvenstedt.

2016

RTM · Tibia

THM joins forces with Italy's 3T. The same year at Eurobike it unveils the new RTM Fibula and a 78g Tibia stem at 100mm.

2020

Schmolke Carbon

Schmolke Carbon, a German carbon component maker, takes over THM. The two companies run production, sales and customer support independently.

2026
